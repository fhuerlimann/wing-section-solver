What is being solved

The incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in two dimensions, with constant density and an optional physical viscosity:

∂u/∂t + (u·∇)u = −∇p/ρ + ν∇²u    ∇·u = 0

Air enters from the left at speed U. The top and bottom walls are free-slip, the right edge is an open outlet, and obstacles are no-slip. Forces are integrated from cell pressures only, so drag excludes skin friction. The upwind advection adds numerical viscosity, so the true Reynolds number is lower than the nominal value shown.

One time step

  1. Mark solid cells and give their faces the obstacle's velocity (zero for drawn walls).
  2. Diffuse velocity implicitly with Gauss–Seidel sweeps when ν > 0.
  3. Project: successive over-relaxation drives the divergence of every fluid cell to zero and accumulates pressure.
  4. Advect velocity and dye semi-Lagrangian: trace each sample backwards along the flow and interpolate bilinearly.
  5. Sum pressure on solid faces to get drag and lift; count lift oscillations to estimate the shedding frequency.
